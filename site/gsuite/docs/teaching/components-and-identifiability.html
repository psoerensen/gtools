5. Multiple components, correlated effects and identifiability

Advanced notes: contents · Abbreviations

1 From one random term to a covariance decomposition

For independent components, \(V=\sum_k Z_kK_kZ_k^\top+R\). This representation accommodates additive genetics, common family environment, herd, permanent individual environment and additional genetic kernels. The component label does not determine its covariance or imply that it can be estimated separately from all other terms.

A fixed herd effect removes arbitrary mean differences between the observed herds. A random herd effect instead specifies a covariance and shrinkage rule. These choices can alter both estimable genetic contrasts and the allocation of variation. A large number of levels is not, by itself, a reason to treat an effect as random.

2 Correlated direct and maternal genetic effects

The quantitative-genetic decomposition of maternal and direct effects is developed in Willham (1972). Sparse restricted maximum likelihood (REML) computation for a direct/maternal animal model is illustrated by Johnson and Thompson (1995).

Let \(d\) be direct effects and \(m\) maternal effects on the same animal domain:

\[ y=X\beta+Z_d d+Z_m m+e,\qquad \operatorname{Var}\begin{pmatrix}d\\m\end{pmatrix} =\begin{bmatrix}\sigma_d^2A&\sigma_{dm}A\\ \sigma_{dm}A&\sigma_m^2A\end{bmatrix}. \]

The observed covariance includes the cross terms \(\sigma_{dm}(Z_dAZ_m^\top+Z_mAZ_d^\top)\). Omitting them is an independence assumption, not a change of solver. Positive semidefiniteness requires \(\sigma_{dm}^2\leq\sigma_d^2\sigma_m^2\). A genetic correlation is undefined when either marginal variance is zero.

Maternal permanent environment is a separate identity-based dam component; it is not a replacement for correlated maternal genetics. Pedigree connections, which animals have records and which dams have several offspring influence how well these effects can be separated.

3 Repeated records separate some environmental sources

In a repeatability model, \(y_{it}=x_{it}^\top\beta+a_i+c_i+e_{it}\). If residuals are independent, two records from the same animal share \(\sigma_a^2A_{ii}+\sigma_c^2\); records from different animals share \(\sigma_a^2A_{ij}\). Repetition and relatedness thus contribute different information about permanent environment and genetics. Serial residual correlation can explain additional within-animal covariance and must be considered when interpreting that separation.

With exactly one visit and identical incidence, an unrestricted individual component and residual component enter through their sum. The four-trait benchmark remains a valid supplied-covariance prediction problem, but does not by itself establish that its environmental split can be estimated.

4 Diagnosing confounding

The information-based argument concerns local identification under regularity conditions (Rothenberg, 1971); boundary and singular cases require separate care. The component covariance identities below also provide direct algebraic counterexamples to identification.

For linear variance components \(V=\sum_k\theta_k B_k\), consider whether the projected matrices \(PB_kP\) have distinguishable directions. Exact dependence produces non-identification; near dependence can cause large component standard errors and sensitivity to constraints. A pedigree kernel close to identity may be difficult to distinguish from an individual identity component in a limited design.

Distinct parameter estimates are not evidence that the design identified them. Examine information eigenvalues, profiles and sensitivity of total covariance and predictions to alternative decompositions. Predictions of a sum of effects may be stable while its named components are unstable.

5 Structured alternatives and scientific restrictions

A block Cholesky parameterization ensures positive definiteness in the interior. A factor model, diagonal covariance or fixed variance ratio instead imposes substantive restrictions and reduces parameter count. Constraints should be chosen from the scientific design and validation, not solely to make a failed optimizer report convergence.

At a zero component variance, the effect can be absent from the active model. For correlated components, boundary reduction also changes covariance rank. Boundary inference must account for that rank change and the resulting constraints on covariance parameters.

5.1 Information added by annotations and measurement systems

A new annotation label does not necessarily create a new observed covariance direction. Under a variance-function model, local information concerns the projected derivatives \(P V_l P\) for annotation coefficients, after accounting for competing scale and environmental parameters. Strongly correlated or nearly identical feature kernels can leave enrichment weakly determined while total prediction remains stable (Rothenberg, 1971).

For sensor traits, analogous confounding can occur between genetic loadings, permanent environment and device effects. Additional frames from the same animal improve some measurement precision but do not replace relatives, connected devices or repeated biological occasions. Replicated crop designs and related-animal data can separate covariance through different information. A representation learned under one design needs a new identification argument under another. Functional models and phenomics give the corresponding constructions.

6 Specification record

For each component state its domain, incidence, covariance or precision, scale, correlation with other components, parameters supplied versus estimated, and prediction target. Identify which features of the design distinguish it from the remaining components. This is especially important for overlapping genomic annotations and genetic/non-genetic family effects.

Implementation in gsuite

Multiple independent pedigree/identity components and correlated channels sharing a relationship are implemented in the development component R interface. The pedigree tutorial includes repeatability and direct/maternal examples; standalone models expose further incidence and covariance constructions. General cross-component covariance is not supported by the component R contract. None of these constructors resolves the identification problems described in this chapter.
