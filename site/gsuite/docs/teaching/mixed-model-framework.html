1. General framework, targets and assumptions

Advanced notes: contents

1 Scope and inferential targets

The statistical framework follows the mixed-model prediction literature (Henderson, 1975; Robinson, 1991), with likelihood and Bayesian treatments developed in Sorensen and Gianola (2002).

This reference assumes working knowledge of linear models, multivariate normal distributions and matrix algebra. Its purpose is to make the statistical and computational choices in a genetic evaluation explicit. For a gradual introduction, see Mixed-model foundations.

The relationship and marker-model families are developed in Chapter 4.

Three questions must be separated: which joint distribution defines the model, which quantities are estimated or predicted, and which numerical representation is used to compute them. Identical likelihoods need not imply identical named effect decompositions; identical predictions need not imply identical uncertainty calculations.

Let the observed-data model be

\[ y=X\beta+\sum_{j=1}^s Z_j u_j+e, \qquad u_j\sim N(0,K_j(\theta)),\quad e\sim N(0,R(\theta)), \]

with mutually independent random components. Collect \(Z=[Z_1,\ldots,Z_s]\) and \(K=\operatorname{blockdiag}(K_1,\ldots,K_s)\). Then

\[ V=ZKZ^\top+R. \]

The covariance parameters \(\theta\) may be supplied or estimated. Unless stated otherwise, \(R\) and \(K\) are positive definite, \(X\) is full column rank, and the covariances are treated as known. The distinction matters: plug-in prediction after REML is not a linear function of the original data in general, and its usual plug-in uncertainty omits covariance-parameter estimation uncertainty.

2 Information, estimability and connectedness

For the relationship between local identification and information at regular points, see Rothenberg (1971). Singular boundary cases require separate analysis.

Full rank of \(X\) is sufficient for the formulas below. If \(X\) is rank deficient, only estimable functions of \(\beta\) have invariant estimates. A generalized inverse or an explicit constraint can select coefficients, but does not create information about an unestimable contrast. Software comparisons must align constraints or compare invariant fitted values and contrasts.

Genetic connectedness is also a property of the design and covariance model. If management groups are poorly linked by relatives or shared information, between-group genetic contrasts can have large prediction error even when the equations have a unique numerical solution. Inspect uncertainty for the scientific contrast, not only marginal animal reliabilities.

For covariance parameters, local identifiability can be examined through \(V_i=\partial V/\partial\theta_i\) after removal of fixed-effect directions. If two parameter changes produce the same observed covariance change, their separate interpretation is not supported by these data. Small information eigenvalues indicate weak identification rather than a linear-solver failure.

3 Declaring the target before choosing the model

A breeding value is a transmissible additive component relative to a reference population. A total genotypic value may additionally include dominance and interaction effects. A phenotype includes environmental and residual terms. The same fitted model can supply several targets, but their prediction error variances and practical interpretations differ.

Write a scalar target as \(t=l_\beta^\top\beta+l_u^\top u\). Specifying \(l\) requires the individual, trait, environmental condition and time point, where relevant. Predictions for a new herd or a future time point require a joint covariance model connecting that target to the training observations. A label not seen in training is not automatically the same target as a known level with no response.

4 Genetic and non-genetic components

The distinction is substantive rather than algebraic. A pedigree, genomic or identity kernel can be used inside a covariance component, but the scientific meaning comes from the effect definition and design. Herd, litter, maternal environment and permanent individual environment can induce substantial covariance without being additive genetic variation.

Independence between components is a modelling assumption. Direct and maternal genetic effects, for example, can be correlated; their cross-covariance belongs in the joint \(K\). Calling two terms separate effects does not imply zero covariance between them.

5 Assumptions that need an explicit statement

The conditional mean, covariance structure and reference population jointly define the analysis. Record how covariates enter the mean, whether environmental effects are fixed or random, whether residuals are independent, and whether relationships are assumed known. State the observation/selection assumptions needed to use the likelihood in the observed sample. Distributional assumptions beyond means and covariances matter for likelihood inference and posterior probabilities even when a linear prediction formula remains available.

Binary, ordinal, count and survival responses generally require observation models beyond the Gaussian LMM developed here. A linear analysis of such a response is a separate approximation, with a response-scale interpretation that must not be confused with a latent-scale genetic model.
