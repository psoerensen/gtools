Advanced notes: contents

1 Numerical acceptance and scientific agreement

Backward error, conditioning and iterative refinement are treated by Higham (2002). A residual criterion is a numerical diagnostic, not a biological model-checking procedure.

For \(C\hat x=b\), record the original-system residual \(r=b-C\hat x\). A useful normwise backward-error diagnostic is

\[ \eta=\frac{\|r\|}{\|C\|\,\|\hat x\|+\|b\|}. \]

A small residual does not by itself guarantee small coefficient error when the system is ill conditioned. The identity

\[ x-\hat x=C^{-1}r \]

shows why scaling and conditioning matter. A solve in transformed variables must be checked after reconstructing the original target, under the declared original-system acceptance criterion.

An SPD preconditioner used solely to accelerate PCG preserves the target equations; finite stopping error still depends on convergence. Refinement changes how accurately a backend solve is performed. Neither should be confused with adding a ridge to \(K\), which changes the statistical model. Residual acceptance, variance-component optimizer convergence and scientific predictive validity are three separate assessments.

Software comparisons should align response records, effects, pedigree base, genotype coding, covariance scales and prediction targets. Compare all target values by external ID and trait, report maximum error and RMSE, and retain the prespecified tolerance result. Practical equivalence is a scientific judgment about the size of differences; it does not retrospectively turn a failed numerical gate into a pass. Report end-to-end and phase timings separately, alongside hardware, allocated physical cores, memory measurement method and numerical acceptance.

2 Statistical diagnostics after an accepted solve

Inspect observed patterns, influential groups, residual distributions and variance heterogeneity against the model. Conditional residuals \(y-X\hat\beta-Z\hat u\) have covariance shaped by fitting and shrinkage; they are not independent draws with covariance \(R\). Standardized or whitened diagnostics must use the appropriate fitted residual covariance or a stated approximation. Marginal and conditional residuals address different questions.

A good numerical residual does not diagnose omitted management effects, incorrect parentage or a misspecified residual process. Large scientific residuals do not necessarily imply a failed equation solve either.

3 Validation aligned with deployment

Simulation, validation design and benchmarking in genomic prediction are discussed by Daetwyler et al. (2013). Multitrait-specific validation leakage and remedies are studied by Runcie and Cheng (2019).

A random animal split mostly tests prediction for animals with the relationships and information retained in that split. Forward-generation validation tests a different task; leave-family, leave-population and leave-environment designs answer other questions. Specify what information would genuinely be available at prediction time, including relatives’ future records and environmental covariates.

Marker selection, phenotype-derived weights, hyperparameter tuning and covariance estimation must be fitted within the training procedure for the claimed validation design. If target genotypes are allowed in frequency or kernel construction, state that transductive information explicitly rather than describing the exercise as entirely independent prediction.

4 Accuracy, calibration and uncertainty

In simulation, compare predictions to known latent breeding values. In field data those values are not observed: correlation with phenotype is not genetic accuracy, and heritability-based adjustments require assumptions. Predictive correlation is insensitive to additive shifts and multiplicative dispersion, so also assess bias, regression calibration and prediction error on a clearly specified target.

For held-out phenotypes, mean squared error includes unexplained environmental and residual variation. For breeding values, reliability describes a model-based uncertainty measure rather than an automatically observed out-of-sample accuracy. Interval coverage should be checked against the target actually covered, with repeated simulation or an appropriate external design.

Partial-versus-whole-data prediction comparisons, including the (Legarra and Reverter, 2018), provide additional validation tools under their assumptions. They do not make true breeding values directly observable or eliminate the effects of an incorrect covariance model.

5 Comparing software and models

First establish the same data, allele coding, relationship base, covariance scales and prediction target. Align external animal IDs and trait order; exclude marker coefficients when comparing animal genetic values. Check complete coverage, duplicates and nonfinite outputs before error summaries. Report maximum absolute error, per-target RMSE and a prespecified absolute/ relative gate. Agreement on predictions alone does not prove equal likelihoods, PEVs or variance estimates.

Then compare setup, factorization, iteration, reconstruction and output time, plus end-to-end time, on documented hardware. Use repeated runs where practical to assess variability. Different stopping criteria and different nodes limit causal claims about algorithmic speed. A smaller time per iteration can coexist with a larger complete runtime.

Finally distinguish two conclusions: equivalent-model software qualification and predictive model comparison. The former asks whether implementations solve the same problem; the latter asks whether a model improves inference or prediction for a scientifically relevant population.

6 A report that can be interpreted later

Preserve the scientific specification, input identities, code/runtime versions, parameter estimates or supplied covariances, numerical status and uncertainty method. State the validation design and what was learned inside each training set. Keep unsuccessful numerical gates and unsupported uncertainty requests visible; practical agreement is not a replacement for recording those results.
