7. Missing data, selection and population structure

Advanced notes: contents

1 The observed-data model

Let \(S\) select the observed entries from a conceptual complete response vector. Then \(y_o=Sy\), \(X_o=SX\), \(Z_o=SZ\) and \(V_o=SVS^\top\). In a Gaussian likelihood, unobserved responses are integrated out: their absence is not represented by a zero response. A missing trait can coexist with observed traits for the same animal and residual unit.

For a residual covariance block, use the covariance submatrix for the observed traits and then invert or factor that block. Selecting a block of the complete precision is generally different: it represents conditional rather than marginal information. The same distinction appears in pedigree Schur complements.

Irregular longitudinal records preserve their actual times and block identities. Rows should not be inserted with arbitrary zero values to create a balanced grid.

2 Which missingness process can be ignored?

Let \(D\) describe response observation indicators. The observed likelihood is proportional to

\[ \int p(y_o,y_m\mid\theta)\, p(D\mid y_o,y_m,\psi)\,dy_m. \]

Under missing at random, the observation mechanism depends on observed rather than unobserved responses, conditional on the variables included in the analysis. With distinct model and missingness parameters, the likelihood for \(\theta\) can ignore that mechanism in the usual ignorable setting. Bayesian ignorability also needs the appropriate prior factorization. See (RUBIN, 1976).

Missing completely at random is stronger: observation does not depend on response values. Missing not at random permits dependence on unobserved values. The observation pattern alone cannot generally establish which mechanism holds. A numerical method’s ability to accept incomplete records is therefore not proof of valid inference under informative recording or dropout.

Missing covariates and unknown genotypes require their own treatment. Deleting responses and imputing predictors are different operations, with different assumptions and potential uncertainty.

3 Selection and selective genotyping

Selection based on recorded information can sometimes be handled by including the relevant information and modelling it correctly. Selection on omitted historical records, latent performance or unmodelled criteria can make the observed sample likelihood inappropriate for the claimed population inference. There is no universal statement that BLUP or single-step automatically removes all selection bias.

Selective genotyping changes which animals contribute genomic information. Single-step models connect genotyped and non-genotyped animals through pedigree, but require a defensible pedigree/genomic base and adequate modelling of the information driving selection. Check parentage, cohort representation and whether the recorded pedigree omits important selected ancestors.

The foundational selection argument is developed by Henderson (1975). It relies on assumptions; applying its equations is not a substitute for checking the recording and selection history.

4 Animals without phenotypes, genotypes or known parents

An animal without a phenotype may remain a valid prediction target or an informative pedigree link. An animal without genotypes is not equivalent to an animal whose centered dosages are all zero. A genomic prediction for that animal requires a joint model, such as pedigree/genomic single-step, or another explicitly justified imputation and uncertainty model.

Unknown parents define founder assumptions relative to a base population. Genetic groups and metafounders are ways to express particular base differences; they are not generic remedies for arbitrary missing pedigree. Their covariance, constraints and genomic compatibility must be specified and qualified.

5 Population structure and transport

Allele frequencies, LD, management systems and genetic effects can differ between training and target populations. Adjusting \(G\) to pedigree moments aligns selected covariance summaries; it does not establish portability of marker effects or eliminate every population difference.

Specify whether the intended task is prediction within families, across families, across generations, across breeds or in new environments. That task should determine both the reference assumptions and validation split.
