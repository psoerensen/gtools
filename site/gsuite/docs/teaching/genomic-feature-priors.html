11. Genomic-feature information and prior models

Advanced mixed-model notes — first draft

Published

November 1, 2026

Advanced notes: contents

1 Biological information as part of the statistical model

A genomic feature can be a variant class, gene, pathway, regulatory region, tissue-specific molecular annotation or a quantitative score. Its statistical role must be specified: it may define a covariance component, an expected marker variance, a mixture probability or a predictor measured on each animal. These choices are not interchangeable.

Retain the marker model from Chapter 4:

\[ y=X\beta+ZWm+e. \]

\(W\) contains consistently centered marker dosages. In ordinary SNP-BLUP, \(m\sim N(0,\tau^2I)\). Feature information allows the marker distribution to depend on an annotation design \(B\), with row \(b_j^\top\) describing marker \(j\). The same annotation can be external biological information even when its influence on the trait is estimated from the training phenotypes.

The word “prior” has two uses here. A feature set may be defined before an analysis, without assigning a Bayesian prior to its variance component. A full Bayesian model additionally assigns probability distributions to unknown effects and hyperparameters. Declaring a feature in advance does not mean that its enrichment is known.

2 Feature-specific variance components: GFBLUP

Partition markers into a feature set \(F\) and its complement \(B\). Write

\[ a=W_Fm_F+W_Bm_B=f+r, \]

with independent effects

\[ m_F\sim N(0,\tau_F^2I),\qquad m_B\sim N(0,\tau_B^2I). \]

For positive normalizers \(d_F,d_B\), define \(G_F=W_FW_F^\top/d_F\) and \(G_B=W_BW_B^\top/d_B\). Then

\[ K_a=\sigma_F^2G_F+\sigma_B^2G_B, \qquad \sigma_F^2=d_F\tau_F^2,\quad \sigma_B^2=d_B\tau_B^2. \]

The feature and background variances can be supplied, estimated by REML or assigned hyperpriors. Their interpretation follows the kernel scaling. Equal marker variances correspond to \(\sigma_F^2/d_F=\sigma_B^2/d_B\), not generally to equal component variances. If \(d=d_F+d_B\) under the same normalization convention, ordinary GBLUP is recovered by these matching per-marker variances.

This makes the feature model a direct extension of multiple-component inference. The feature provides a grouping hypothesis; the data determine whether its markers warrant different shrinkage. A small or noisy feature can produce an unstable component estimate, and a boundary estimate need not imply that the underlying biology is absent.

2.1 What does feature enrichment mean?

An effect-size enrichment is \(E_\tau=\tau_F^2/\tau_B^2\). A ratio of total component variances instead includes feature size and normalization. Neither should be reported merely as “the enrichment” without a denominator.

For a declared evaluation population with centered dosage matrices, the average model-implied feature variance is

\[ v_F=\frac{\sigma_F^2}{n}\operatorname{tr}(G_F), \]

and similarly for background. The ratio \(v_F/(v_F+v_B)\) is a prior population-average component share under the independent-effect model. This is distinct from a variance share calculated from fitted predictions. For posterior draws, feature and background effects can become correlated through the data and LD; posterior realized variance decompositions need their cross term.

3 Weighted kernels and continuous annotation-dependent variances

A more general Gaussian model is

\[ m_j\mid\alpha\sim N(0,\tau_j^2),\qquad \log\tau_j^2=\alpha_0+b_j^\top\alpha, \]

which induces \(K_a=W D(\alpha)W^\top\), where \(D\) is diagonal. This general variance-regression formulation accommodates continuous scores and overlapping annotations. Positive variance is automatic, while regularization of \(\alpha\) controls extreme weights and weakly identified annotation effects.

Fixed external weights define one covariance. Learning weights by likelihood or empirical Bayes defines a covariance-estimation problem. Assigning priors to \(\alpha\) and integrating over its uncertainty defines a hierarchical Bayesian problem. Plugging learned weights into a final BLUP does not automatically propagate their uncertainty.

Correlated annotation columns make individual coefficients difficult to interpret. Genomic coordinates, annotation version, feature mapping, MAF/LD dependencies and score scaling are part of the model specification.

4 Annotation-dependent mixture distributions

For marker allocation \(c_j\) and mixture variances \(v_k\),

\[ \Pr(c_j=k\mid b_j)=\pi_{jk},\qquad m_j\mid c_j=k\sim N(0,v_k), \]

where a component with \(v_0=0\) is a point mass at zero. Annotation information can change the probability of inclusion, the distribution of nonzero sizes, or both. These mechanisms need not produce the same prediction or genetic architecture estimate as a Gaussian model with a matching second moment.

In the original BayesRC construction, predefined variant classes have class-specific mixture proportions learned from the data. MacLeod and colleagues used this to incorporate biological information into a BayesR-type model. Their real-data findings also show why useful priors do not guarantee large prediction gains: informative enrichment and the variance represented by the feature both matter. (MacLeod et al., 2016).

An overlapping-annotation extension can instead make mixture probabilities a function of \(b_j\), for example through multinomial or sequential probit/logit links. Such links, their coefficient priors and update rules are part of the model. They are not identical to independent Dirichlet updates of disjoint feature-class probabilities.

The prior probability \(\pi_{jk}\), posterior allocation probability and posterior inclusion probability are different quantities. Inclusion depends on the prior and LD; it is not automatically a causal probability. A posterior enrichment estimate is also conditional on the selected feature definitions and competing annotations.

5 Overlapping features: two different constructions

The independent-component covariance sum is a general mixed-model identity (Sorensen and Gianola, 2002). Its identifiability depends on the resulting covariance directions, not simply the number of annotation labels (Rothenberg, 1971).

In an annotation regression, one marker effect has several predictors of its variance or mixture probabilities. In an additive multi-kernel model, a marker can contribute through several independent latent components. These are different decompositions.

For nonnegative membership weights \(q_{jk}\), consider

\[ G_k=\frac{W\operatorname{diag}(q_{\cdot k})W^\top}{d_k},\qquad K_a=\sum_k\sigma_k^2G_k. \]

The equivalent marginal marker variance is \(\tau_j^2=\sum_k\sigma_k^2q_{jk}/d_k\). A marker in two features receives both contributions. This is mathematically valid, but the resulting component variances are not automatically unique biological attributions. Identical or nearly identical kernels can be unidentifiable or weakly identifiable.

Nonnegative weighted kernels preserve positive semidefiniteness. Signed annotation scores cannot simply be inserted as diagonal kernel weights without checking that property. Centering a score is appropriate as a regression covariate but need not be appropriate as a covariance weight.

6 Molecular evidence and its uncertainty

Feature definitions can use coding consequence, regulatory activity, eQTL, sQTL, expression, pathways or fine-mapping results. CattleGTEx provides a multi-tissue regulatory resource that can inform such definitions. (Liu et al., 2022).

A tissue-specific eQTL annotation is variant-level information. An expression measurement on an individual animal is a different predictor, potentially affected by environment, disease and the phenotype itself. A model using the latter must state when expression is measured and whether it is available at selection time. It does not automatically predict the same additive breeding value as a genotype-only model.

GWAS association, colocalization and experimental functional validation also provide different evidence. LD can make several variants or genes appear informative for one locus. Combining an annotation with a GWAS statistic from the same animals does not create independent prior evidence.

Treat uncertain or tissue-mismatched annotations as hypotheses rather than known causal labels. Sensitivity to alternative mappings, irrelevant tissues and matched random features helps determine whether a result reflects useful biology or generic genome structure.

7 Multitrait and single-step extensions

For feature \(k\), a multitrait Gaussian extension has \(\operatorname{Var}(a_k)=\Sigma_k\otimes G_k\) in trait-major order. Feature-specific trait correlations allow a region to affect several traits differently from the background. They also increase parameter count and require enough independent information to distinguish the kernels and trait covariance structures.

A Bayesian extension can use shared versus trait-specific inclusion, or mixtures of multivariate effect covariances. Shared inclusion does not force identical effect sizes or signs. Sharing annotations does not by itself establish that the same causal variant acts on every trait.

In single-step, define the complete genotyped covariance induced by the feature model and the conditional relationship to non-genotyped animals. Compatibility, reference adjustment and residual pedigree variation still matter. Replacing ordinary SNP shrinkage by a feature prior without revisiting the remaining latent terms is not proof of equivalence to a desired animal-effect model. Their joint covariance, identifiability and inferential properties must be derived and assessed for the combined model.

8 Qualification: prediction, estimation and biology

Genomic benchmarking should match the intended information and deployment population (Daetwyler et al., 2013). Leakage from correlated-trait information is a particular concern in multivariate validation (Runcie and Cheng, 2019).

Start with an unannotated model using the same markers and information. Then compare fixed feature weights, estimated feature variances and a suitable mixture model where the scientific question warrants it. Include a background component so that an imperfect feature need not explain all genetic variation.

Assess informative, irrelevant and deliberately misspecified annotations. Controls should consider feature size, MAF, LD, gene density and annotation overlap: an arbitrary random SNP set need not be an adequate null for a gene-based feature. When many features are searched, selection and tuning belong inside training; an untouched outer validation is needed for the selected procedure’s performance claim.

Report prediction accuracy and calibration separately from component variance, enrichment and feature selection stability. Feature variance is not evidence that an entire pathway is causal. Within-family validation can conceal or exaggerate portability, so include the populations and generations relevant to the intended application. Estimation uncertainty and numerical acceptance remain necessary even when prediction differences are small.
