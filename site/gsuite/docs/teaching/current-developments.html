10. Current developments in animal-breeding prediction and estimation

Advanced mixed-model notes — first draft

Published

November 1, 2026

Advanced notes: contents

1 What should count as a methodological advance?

The central tasks remain prediction of genetic merit, estimation of genetic and environmental variation, and quantification of uncertainty. Developments change the information available, the assumptions connecting populations and traits, or the computational cost of inference. A more elaborate model is useful when it improves the relevant scientific decision, not simply because it contains more markers or parameters.

This is a selected perspective as of November 2026, rather than an exhaustive ranking of software or methods. It connects research directions to the prediction, estimation and validation foundations already developed.

Distinguish three outcomes. Predictive improvement concerns performance on the intended target population. Improved estimation concerns the parameters and uncertainty describing that population. Biological insight concerns the interpretation of variants, features and mechanisms. These outcomes can diverge: highly correlated markers can predict well without identifying a causal variant, and an informative feature can improve variance partitioning without materially changing within-family prediction.

Direction Scientific opportunity Main issue to establish
Population-scale single-step Use pedigree, genotypes and records jointly Compatibility, selection history and feasible computation
Sequence and functional information Prioritize information closer to causal variation Annotation quality, imputation and independent validation
Across-population prediction Extend evaluation beyond close relatives LD, frequency, effect and environment differences
Multitrait and longitudinal phenotyping Use correlated outcomes and repeated records Measurement quality, identification and target availability
G×E and robustness Predict responses to changing environments Environmental support and extrapolation
Large-scale covariance estimation Fit the model rather than simplify it for convenience Trace/solve cost, selection and parameter uncertainty
Nonlinear prediction Capture departures from additive Gaussian assumptions Appropriate baselines, leakage and reproducibility

2 Prediction: retain the joint information while scaling computation

The joint pedigree/genomic framework and its computational connections are reviewed by Legarra et al. (2014); a large-genotype APY application is given by Garcia et al. (2020).

Single-step evaluation places genotyped and non-genotyped animals in a joint relationship model. Modern computational work asks how to retain that target while avoiding dense storage or excessive repeated solves. The alternatives include marker-effect representations, sparse conditional pedigree operations, retained factorizations, matrix-free products and approximate relationship constructions. The relevant distinctions were derived in Chapter 4.

An improved representation can preserve the exact covariance model. An APY approximation to the target relationship need not. A cheaper preconditioner may leave the target unchanged while altering the cost of convergence. These must be assessed separately, using original-system acceptance and the final scientific prediction target.

The practical objective is not minimum time per PCG iteration. A useful evaluation accounts for preparation, factorization, preconditioner setup, operator applications, reconstruction, uncertainty and output. It also checks whether changes in computational policy remain reliable for other pedigree structures, trait counts and blending rules. See Chapter 8.

3 Prediction across generations, breeds and populations

The roles of relationship, LD and cosegregation information are analyzed by Habier et al. (2013). Cross-breed prediction and heterogeneous marker shrinkage are evaluated by Erbe et al. (2012).

Within a closely related population, markers carry information about both realized relationships and association with causal variants. Transfer to more distant populations changes the usefulness of those signals. A larger reference population can help, but adding samples from another population is not automatically beneficial under a model that assumes identical effects.

One modelling template is

\[ m^{(b)}=m^{(0)}+\delta^{(b)}, \]

where \(m^{(0)}\) is shared and \(\delta^{(b)}\) represents population-specific deviation. This is a hierarchical assumption to estimate and validate, not an automatic correction for population differences. A multivariate model with correlated population-specific effects expresses a related idea.

Transfer can fail through allele alignment, imputation, LD mismatch, frequency differences, environmental change or genuinely different effects. Functional information may make some marker information more portable, but should be tested in forward-generation or across-population validation rather than inferred from a random split of close relatives.

4 Sequence information and biological prioritization

Sequence data increase the opportunity to include causal variation, but also increase the number of correlated, rare and imperfectly imputed predictors. Adding all variants under unchanged shrinkage need not yield the best use of the information. Possible responses include feature-specific kernels, annotation-dependent effect distributions and explicit treatment of known large-effect loci.

In a 2025 simulated livestock study, Yang and colleagues compared GBLUP, two-component GBLUP, support-vector regression and random forests with varying amounts of known QTL information. The two-component models performed best in that design; additional QTL information did not make the machine-learning methods universally superior. This is evidence about that controlled setting, not a ranking for all livestock traits. (Yang et al., 2025).

The scientific question is therefore how information enters the model: a separate variance component, a changed inclusion probability, a changed slab variance, or an individual-level molecular predictor. These are developed in Chapter 11.

5 Multitrait, longitudinal and environmental information

Random regression and environmental covariance provide established building blocks (Meyer, 1998; Meyer, 2001; Su et al., 2006). The availability of secondary traits at prediction time also changes the appropriate validation design (Runcie and Cheng, 2019).

Repeated measurements and correlated secondary traits can support prediction of difficult or expensive traits. Factor models and random regression reduce the dimension of the covariance representation, while retaining a clear target such as growth trajectory, environmental sensitivity or response at a particular age. Their assumptions and identification requirements remain those in Chapter 6.

High-throughput measurements create additional problems: changing sensor calibration, measurement error, selective recording and availability of the secondary phenotype at deployment. A secondary phenotype recorded only after the selection decision cannot be treated as if it were available beforehand. Prediction of latent biological performance and prediction of a noisy sensor measurement are different tasks.

For robustness, specify whether the target is performance at an environment, a slope, a contrast between environments, or an index combining performance and stability. A reaction-norm slope is model- and scale-dependent; it is not a universal biological definition of resilience. New environments outside the observed range require an explicit extrapolation assessment.

6 Estimation: fitting the covariance model at evaluation scale

Covariance estimation requires more than solving the prediction equations. REML derivatives contain trace terms and quadratic forms; an optimizer may evaluate these repeatedly at trial covariances. Exact selected-inverse and selected-solve methods, randomized traces, Monte Carlo REML and matrix-free operations address different parts of this cost.

Bermann and colleagues extended Monte Carlo REML to large single-step models in 2025. Their reported studies include comparison with exact traces in a smaller multivariate model and an application with millions of pedigree animals. The important development is the ability to estimate covariances with joint genomic information while reducing inverse-related work, rather than assuming that a prediction solver alone completes REML. (Bermann et al., 2025).

The balance between deterministic and stochastic work needs explicit accounting. Probe variability, inaccurate inner solves and optimizer error are separate. A method can preserve the likelihood target while estimating its derivatives approximately. Its finite-run parameter uncertainty should not be confused with an exact optimization result.

7 Estimation under selection and changing genetic parameters

An estimate of variation in a founder reference population need not equal the variation among selected animals in a recent cohort. Selection changes allele frequencies and can induce LD; drift, trait definition and environmental change also matter. The desired time-specific parameter must be defined before interpreting differences between estimation procedures.

A 2026 study examined genetic-parameter estimation via predictivity (GPP) under simulated genomic selection and changing trait relationships. This illustrates an emerging alternative to full covariance optimization. Its accuracy formulas, validation population and target time slice require scrutiny; it is not simply another optimizer for the same REML objective. The paper also reports direction-dependent correlation estimates in its experiments. (Gowane et al., 2026).

Predictivity-based quantities, realized cohort parameters and historical-base variance components should not be compared as though they necessarily have identical estimands. Likewise, optimizing predictive correlation does not automatically identify a unique covariance decomposition.

8 Nonlinear and machine-learning methods

Kernels, trees, neural networks and hybrid models offer different ways to represent nonlinear relationships. Their usefulness depends on trait architecture, sample size, information available and deployment population. A nonlinear predictor of phenotype is not automatically an additive breeding value or an estimator of transmissible genetic variance.

Three comparisons are needed: against a well-tuned additive baseline, against a biologically informed linear/Bayesian baseline, and against the proposed method under the same information restrictions. Hyperparameter selection, variant selection and any representation learned from phenotypes belong inside training. Report calibration, reproducibility and computation alongside accuracy. The result of the Yang et al. simulation above is a useful reason to avoid assuming that algorithmic complexity guarantees improvement.

9 Integrating feature information with modern evaluation

Feature/background covariance components connect directly to the multiple- component framework. Annotation-dependent marker mixtures connect to hierarchical Bayesian regression. Their integration with single-step, multitrait and longitudinal models requires a complete joint specification: the effect distribution, pedigree/genomic connection and prediction target must remain consistent. This is a statistical development question rather than a restriction to one software package.

A useful qualification sequence is to verify the induced covariance on small examples, test prediction and estimation separately, assess misspecified or uninformative features, and then evaluate representative large data. The advancement claim should say whether the gain is prediction, estimation, interpretability or computation. All four are valuable; they need different evidence.

10 Unresolved questions and the evidence a next method needs

A research direction becomes a useful method when its target, assumptions and advancement criterion are explicit. The following questions connect the existing foundations to future work; they are not claims that their solutions are already established.

Question Statistical development Evidence needed
How should uncertain annotations influence shrinkage? A hierarchy separating annotation quality from trait enrichment Sensitivity to irrelevant/misspecified features and independent prediction
What transfers between populations? Shared and population-specific effect distributions Forward-generation and across-population calibration at matched information
How should changing genetic variation be estimated? A declared cohort/base estimand and a selection-aware model Recovery of that estimand under controlled selection and recording
How can many traits and environments be modelled? Structured covariance with identifiable directions Covariance recovery, target uncertainty and validation in new conditions
How can precision be allocated at scale? Separate numerical and stochastic error control for scores and PEV Exact small references, independent precision assessment and solve accounting

The historical perspective explains how earlier methods addressed related information problems. The algorithmic reference and qualification framework provide the next two steps: derive the computation and establish the scope of its evidence.
